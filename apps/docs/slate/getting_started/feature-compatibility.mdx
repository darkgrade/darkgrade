## Operations by camera

Every operation has one name on every camera (see the [Camera API](/slate/getting_started/camera)).
This table says which drivers implement it. Whether the attached body can do it now depends on its
model, firmware, mode and lens: `getCapabilities().operations` answers that, with the reason when
it cannot, and a call it cannot make fails with `CameraUnsupportedError` before anything is sent.

```typescript
const { operations, controls, lens } = await camera.getCapabilities()
for (const [name, availability] of Object.entries(operations ?? {}))
    console.log(name, availability?.supported ? 'available' : availability?.reason)
console.log(controls.photoFormat?.allowedValues, lens?.focalLengthMillimetres)
if (operations?.getLiveViewFrame?.options?.overlay?.supported) await camera.getLiveViewFrame({ overlay: true })
```

The table describes implemented driver paths. Check the connected body's current
capabilities, firmware, mode and transport before offering a control. The camera tables
above record model-specific evidence. "Tested by us" means a Darkgrade bench or Studio
session on that body; "Public capture" means a recording or report someone else published,
which shows what the body sent but is not a Darkgrade test. Advertised operations and
simulated scenarios do not certify a camera family or >99% long-run reliability.

## Settings by camera

Each of the 30 settings has a typed getter and setter and the same canonical values on every
camera. Sony's Creative Look, Canon's Picture Style and Nikon's Picture Control are all
`setPictureStyle`; Sony's Flexible Spot is a `Spot` focus area. This table says which drivers map
a setting to the camera's own property. A mapped setting appears in `controls` only when the
attached body reports it in its current mode, and offers only the values the body offers now.

| Setting | Generic PTP and Olympus | Sony | Nikon | Canon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shutter speed, aperture, ISO, exposure mode, exposure compensation, metering, white balance, focus mode | ✅ when advertised | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Photo / video (`setPhotoVideoMode`) | ❌ | ✅ <sup>12</sup> | ✅ | ✅ <sup>13</sup> |
| Drive mode | ✅ when advertised | ✅ | ✅ photo mode | ✅ |
| Colour temperature, look (`setPictureStyle`) | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Picture profile | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Focus area | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ |
| Subject detection, detection subject, eye selection | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Photo format | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ✅ |
| Photo size | ✅ when advertised | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Aspect ratio, JPEG quality | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| RAW compression | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ photo mode | ❌ |
| Flash | ✅ when advertised | ✅ | ✅ photo mode | ❌ |
| Video format | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ <sup>14</sup> | ✅ |

| Audio recording | ❌ | ❌ <sup>15</sup> | ❌ | ✅ |

Every setter accepts only values the attached camera offers now, writes once and resolves to the
value read back. A value the camera refuses is `CameraRefusedError` and nothing changed; a write
the camera never shows is `CameraOutcomeUnknownError` and is never resent.

```typescript
await camera.setFocusMode('AF-S')
await camera.setPhotoFormat('RAW+JPEG')
await camera.setAspectRatio('3:2')
console.log(await camera.setPictureStyle('Standard'))
```

## Raw vendor access for bench work

Vendor inventories, Sony buttons and menu sessions, Canon network state, magnification and pan,
Nikon vendor property codes and raw events are protocol access on `protocolSession(camera)` from
the unsupported `@darkgrade/slate/protocol` entry. They follow the vendors' own names and are not
confirmed the way `Camera` operations are:

```typescript
import { protocolSession } from '@darkgrade/slate/protocol'

const session = protocolSession(camera)
if (session.vendorId === 0x054c) console.log((await session.getSonyPropertyStates()).length)
if (session.vendorId === 0x04a9) console.log(session.getCanonNetworkState().communicationMode)
```

Sony's inventory preserves its mode-aware availability flags: an enabled flag of `2` is
display-only in the current body mode. `setCanonNetworkCommunicationMode(value)` accepts only a
mode advertised by that camera. `getNikonVendorPropertyCodes()` enumerates advertised extended
property IDs (355 on the tested Z6 III); unknown properties are not made writable. Nikon property
access and event polling fall back to legacy operations only on `OperationNotSupported`
(`0x2005`); a property refusal (`0x200a`) remains a refusal.

## Known behaviors

- **Canon keep-alive.** A Canon session sends `KeepDeviceOn` every ten seconds when the body advertises it, so the camera does not sleep mid-session. Pass `new Camera({ keepAlive: false })` to turn this off. No power setting saved on the body is changed.
- **Canon HDMI output.** The tested EOS 80D resets its HDMI live-view mode when its PTP session closes; Slate manages live-view output itself.
- **EOS R6 Mark III movie lever.** With the lever at Movie, the body cannot be put in still mode over PTP (firmware 1.0.1). Set the lever on the camera.
- **Sony files over 4 GiB.** `ObjectInfo` reports the size as `0xffffffff` for these. Slate reads the real size from the object's `ObjectSize` property; a 5.1 GB FX2 original transferred and decoded in full. The α6700 splits recordings at 4 GB instead.

## Notes

1. The earliest versions of PTP date back to 2002 and this was not included in the specification (perhaps not thought of as necessary/useful/possible on the first wave of digital still cameras).

2. Same as (1) above

3. Nikon cameras differentiate between "photo mode" and "video mode" with an on-camera hardware switch and do not typically allow capture of (a) videos while in photo mode or (b) photos while in video mode. There are two workarounds we support:
    - You accept this limitation and get full feature support for photo OR video, but not both at the same time, via the hardware switch. This is optimal if you don't plan to do hybrid shooting within the same session.
    - We allow you to do both at the same time in either switch mode, however when you are capturing in the "wrong" mode" (e.g. you start recording a video while in photo mode), the on-screen display on your camera will be blank and say "Connected to Computer."

4. A physical α6700 test found no advertised persistent USB-mode property or `USBConnectionModeRequest` control. Sending the unadvertised request returned PTP `OK` but the next reconnect still opened the mode chooser; requesting the advertised OSD image restarted the body into its pre-PTP HID mode. With USB unplugged, setting **Setup → USB → USB Connection Mode → Remote Shoot (PC Remote)** on the body was then verified to make the next connection enumerate automatically without a prompt. PTP cannot configure the mode that must be selected before the PTP interface exists.

5. The physical α6700 verified mode-aware focus, still-format, movie-format, and aspect-ratio round trips (AF-A/AF-S, JPEG/RAW, XAVC S HD/XAVC HS 4K, 3:2/4:3), restoring each. White balance was display-only in the tested body mode. Remote zoom (`zoomTo`, `startZoom`) confirms optical focal length or Clear Image/digital scale; optical movement requires a power-zoom lens, and some bodies need **Shooting → Zoom → Remote Zoom Speed** enabled. Modern α6700 PC Remote uses Sony access authentication and is not implemented by the standard PTP/IP transport. See [Sony Wi-Fi WIP](/slate/getting_started/sony-wifi-wip). The SDK bridge has separate connection evidence. Canon PTP/IP passes simulator tests but EOS 80D first pairing remains physically unverified.

6. The physical E-PL5 MTP mode publishes battery and date/time but no exposure, focus, or capture property. Darkgrade exposes the capability boundary instead of treating Olympus recognition as proof of control support.

7. Sony Wi-Fi uses Sony's licensed Camera Remote SDK, which is not included in this release and must be installed separately. Slate accepts only the α6700 and FX2 for it (`SonyWifiCameraModel`). The Bluetooth remote (`SonyBluetoothRemote`) does not need the SDK and has been tested only on the α6700. See [Sony Wi-Fi WIP](/slate/getting_started/sony-wifi-wip).

8. Native PTP/IP was verified on the Z6 III (V2.00) on 2026-10-29 with an already-registered smart-device profile: settings, still and movie transfer and preview. First-time pairing is unverified. Wireless status reads work where the body advertises them and on the Z6 III, which omits them from DeviceInfo. Other Nikon bodies are untested over Wi-Fi.

9. α7 V (ILCE-7M5, firmware 1.00) evidence is USB Telemetry from 109 Darkgrade Studio sessions on 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-20. It shows connection and DeviceInfo, the extended property inventory, acknowledged setting writes, live-view frames, still captures (the body reported four new objects; one 304 KB transfer was inspected but not independently decoded), and a movie start that the body's movie state confirmed. Movie stop and clip transfer, card readiness (Slate's single-slot card fallback is α6700-only), reconnect qualification and Wi-Fi are untested. The body answered live-view polls with `0x200F` Access_Denied 186,751 times while no new frame was ready; Slate waits and asks again, so these are not failed commands, but they dominate its diagnostics.

10. Sony bodies in movie mode stayed at UNLOCK on a half-press in bench tests, so `autofocus()` there answers `'unknown'` rather than `'acquired'`. A Sony focus timeout includes the last focus status; a Canon refusal names the SDK reason.

11. On Nikon (validated over USB on Z6 III firmware V2.00) `setFocusPoint` needs ChangeAfArea and live view, and the point is read back from live view. A nudge drives small lens steps in **AF-S**; AF-F can remain busy and MF rejects the drive. The small, medium and large steps are not yet bench-calibrated. Accepted commands and AF-point placement do not establish focus lock: only `'acquired'` does.

12. Sony photo/video is the half the exposure program is in: Movie and S&Q programs are video. A switch selects the same program in the other half (Manual ↔ Movie Manual), and only when the camera advertises it.

13. Canon switches with MovieSelectSwitchOn/Off and reads FixedMovie back. A body whose hardware lever is at Movie stays in video (see the EOS R6 Mark III under Known behaviors).

14. Nikon's movie format, size and frame rate have no decoded properties yet.

15. Sony's AudioRecording property is not mapped: on the α7 V (2026-11-24) a take recorded with it at 0 still carried a sound track. Until its meaning is measured or documented, audio recording is unsupported on Sony rather than a confirmed Off that may still record sound.
